# Finding where headerless appended data stops, with no length field

**When it bites:** unidentified bytes follow an already-solved structure in the same file (past a directory's last entry, after a compressed stream's terminator, beyond a screen's pixel count), with no length prefix, count or terminator marking the end. Also: a "clean render" at an assumed chunk size is your only evidence of what the blob is.

A fixed guessed cutoff, or one good-looking render, is not a boundary. Image data has a small effective byte alphabet (repeated borders and fills). Unrelated data (tables, mixed records, compressed streams, PCM) spreads much closer to uniform. A diversity test tells you *that* the data changes, not *what* it changes into, and not whether the change is a section edge or just a content change inside one format.

**Check / fix:**
- **Structural boundary test:** compute unique-byte count (or entropy, or distinct palette indices once colour decodes) per fixed-size chunk, and require **several consecutive** chunks past the threshold. A busy icon can trip it once. A real transition stays past it. Scan windows with `range(n - K + 1)`, not `range(n - K)`, which misses a boundary in the last K chunks. Sanity check: `uniq = [10]*10 + [50]*5` → `10`.
- **Treat the far side as a new format to identify**, not a tail to keep decoding under the old hypothesis. Don't call a transition a section edge until something independent (a code trace, a table entry, a count) confirms it.
- **Test audio before graphics:** plot the bytes as signed 8-bit min/max per column (attack/decay envelopes with silence between), or use median `|b[i+1]−b[i]|` over a window. If a port exists, `bytes.find(sample ^ 0x80)` against the other platform's archive identifies PCM byte-exactly (signed on Amiga, 0x80-centred on DOS). Check the header fields and loader comments for hints of a second content type.
- **Is it more of the same compressed stream?** Re-run the RLE/PackBits decoder at the boundary. Hundreds of near-zero-length pseudo-streams mean you should read it raw instead (`rle-decode-succeeds-on-garbage.md`).
- **One plane or one record?** A single bitplane of a multi-plane sprite renders as a clean monochrome bitmap on its own. Cross-check the chunk size against `record_size / N_planes` used elsewhere in the engine (mask + 6bpp is common; `amiga-hardware-specifics.md`), and prefer an `N_planes × chunk` decode that renders coherent colour through the confirmed palette. Check boundary counts for clustering at small multiples of N.
- **Record sizes that vary:** look for a *self-tiling* decomposition that consumes a repeating block exactly, not a constant stride.

**Canonical example:** Black Crypt (Amiga, `crawl`). `bcdfb`–`bcdfn` carry 9–19 KB after the monster-sprite RLE stream, logged for sessions as "16x16-style icons". Pass 1 decoded 40-byte chunks as 16×20×1bpp, got clean glyphs, and reported "692 icons" before the noise began. But 40 B is one plane, and the project's other sprite formats are 7 planes (mask + 6bpp EHB). Pass 2 used 280 B/icon and got finished colour art ("92 icons"). The per-file counts (61, 49, 49, 49, 69, …, with 49 = 7×7 in five files) were already a tell. Pass 3 found that only the first **1932 B = 3 × 644 B** is graphics: one wall decoration at three view distances (16×20/16×15/16×11, 7 planes, `280+210+154 = 644`), with nested-rectangle masks shrinking in step. The remaining 7–17 KB is signed 8-bit PCM, matched byte-exact to DOS `clipper.clp` type-4 entries via `XOR 0x80`. One file's tail is tiled 100% by three known samples. The diversity scan had found the transition, but the far side was filed as "noise" inside the icon atlas.

**Variant:** Jungle Strike AGA `heli_sprite` (228 frames, no directory) shows a sharp, reproducible diversity transition at 91%. It is just where the 216 rotation frames end and the 12 explosion frames begin, in the same format with no header or loader reference there.
